## Overfitting risks
Two passes give a noisy per-item estimate; this is mitigated by evaluating only aggregate statistics over n=75 and n=122, never per-item, and by refusing std claims outright. Gen 2's pass was run as a bulk rescore and may differ from pass 2 in batching or session structure, which would inflate the delta and bias against the prediction rather than toward it -- an acceptable asymmetry. The [0.010, 0.035] bracket is anchored on gen 3's mapd 0.0223, which was computed on 47 items that overlap this corpus; the held-out 75-item arm exists precisely to prevent that overlap from confirming the bracket by construction, and only the held-out arm decides. dim_distinct_values of 22-34 across six dimensions on 122 items suggests output quantized to roughly a 0.05 grid, so small deltas may be discretized; if the held-out delta is exactly 0.0 for a large fraction of items, report that fraction and treat near-zero delta as possible caching or determinism rather than as a passing result. Retiring the band/control split means a proximity-specific instability could hide inside a corpus mean; distance-to-bar logging on every disagreeing item is the compensating instrument.

## Rollback trigger
Revert this proposal, restoring the gen 2 configuration, if ANY of: held-out (n=75) within_version_mean_abs_pairwise_delta >= 0.0341, or corpus within_version_mean_abs_pairwise_delta >= 0.045, or notify disagreements > 15% of 122 (> 18 items), or fewer than 50% of disagreeing items lie within 0.05 of their bar (which would mean movement, not proximity, drives decisions and the deleted variance instrumentation was load-bearing). Additionally, if >= 90% of held-out items return a delta of exactly 0.0, treat the run as INVALID rather than passing -- that indicates caching or a shared context, not stability -- and re-run with verified session isolation before drawing any conclusion. No conjunctive all_hold condition applies; absent a listed trigger, the change stands.
